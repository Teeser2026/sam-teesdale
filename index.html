CH 04 · Briefing note

Should Britain programme its money?

● Awaiting feedSam to record · 90 seconds · "If I had ninety seconds with the Chancellor…"
Draft for Sam to rewrite in his own words. Every claim below is sourced; he should be able to defend each one at interview.

The issue

The Bank of England and the Treasury have spent the years since 2023 designing a possible digital pound, and the Bank has said it will announce in 2026 whether to go ahead. Parliament would have to pass a law first.

The worry

"Programmable" money is money with rules attached: what it can be spent on, or when it expires. The Bank has said neither it nor the government would be able to programme digital pounds or restrict how they are spent. The worry is about what a future government might ask for.

The test case: a basic income

Wales gave 644 young people leaving care £1,600 a month (£1,280 after tax) for two years, with no conditions. The evaluation found it worked as a safety net and helped wellbeing, but many struggled when payments stopped, and effects on work and study were mixed.

If a future basic income were paid in programmable money, a government could restrict what it was spent on. That would undo the very freedom the Welsh recipients valued most.

Recommendation

Write any ban on government programming into the law that creates a digital pound, so it can't be quietly changed later.

Sources: Payment Expert, Oct 2026 · Welsh Government evaluation summary, Feb 2026
